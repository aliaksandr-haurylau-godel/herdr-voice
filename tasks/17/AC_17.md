# AC_17

Push-to-talk: hold a key, speak, release, and the text lands in the pane the key
was held over. Issue #17 and its comment are the ticket.

## as-is

**The action exists in name only.** `herdr-plugin.toml` declares `ptt`, and the
client answers `not implemented yet` and exits 69 — `src/main.rs:167`. Nothing
is sent to the daemon on that path. `USAGE` does not list it, and `IMPLEMENTED`
(`src/main.rs:113`) excludes it.

**The daemon already treats it as pane-bound.** `needs_target_pane` matches
`"dictate" | "ptt"` (`src/daemon.rs:41`), so a `ptt` request already parses the
invocation context and requires a focused pane before anything else happens; it
then falls into the same stub, `Reply::Ok(format!("{command}: not implemented
yet"))` (`src/daemon.rs:133`). A test pins the pane requirement
(`src/daemon.rs:1574`).

**A take is driven today by `dictate`, as a toggle, and the daemon keeps no
state between the two halves.** `answer` routes to `dictate` (`src/daemon.rs:151`),
which asks the recorder to start and reads the answer to learn which half this
is: `Started::Began` means a take began, `Started::AlreadyRunning` means this
invocation ends it (`src/daemon.rs:158-163`). All per-take state lives on the
recorder thread as locals — `let mut running: Option<Running> = None;`
(`src/capture.rs:197`) — and the handle to it is a channel behind a mutex
(`src/capture.rs:173`). There is no daemon-side field, and no file, that says a
take is in progress.

**The recorder is a thread that owns the audio device for the daemon's whole
life.** `Recorder::spawn` (`src/capture.rs:190`), `start(target, cwd, agent) ->
Started` (`:237`), `stop() -> Result<Take, CaptureError>` (`:255`). A device
that dies mid-take is reported at the next call, not when it happens
(`src/capture.rs:296-307`, `:341-345`).

**Each keypress is a whole process.** Holding a key starts the client about
twelve times a second (`docs/decisions.md:18`), and the client's reply bound for
anything that is not `dictate` is two seconds — `src/client.rs:32-36`, with a
test naming `ptt` specifically (`src/client.rs:202`).

**There is no timing machinery to build on.** Nothing in the code measures a
hold, an interval, or a gap between requests. The only wall-clock read outside
the model downloader is the take file name, `<millis>-<pid>-<counter>.wav`
(`src/capture.rs:387-392`). No `Instant` is kept across requests.

**There is no `[ptt]` configuration, and its absence is already tested.**
`Config` (`src/config.rs:20-28`) holds `audio`, `stt`, `rewrite`, `ui`,
`delivery`, `context` and nothing else; a test writes a `[ptt]` section with
`release_ms` and asserts the file still loads rather than failing
(`src/config.rs:361-378`).

**Nothing in this repository writes a file atomically.** Every write is a plain
truncating `fs::write` or `wav::write`; the configuration rewrite in
`src/chooser.rs:194-211` is the closest thing and it truncates too. Rule 1 of
the issue has no existing implementation to follow.

**Failures reach the person through three channels that already exist**: the
reply, printed to standard output on success and standard error on failure
(`src/main.rs:168-177`); the journal, written to standard error by
`StderrJournal` (`src/daemon.rs:439`); and a toast through
`herdr notification show` (`src/delivery.rs:222`), gated on `[ui] toasts`.

**Nothing on screen says a recording is running.** The indicator of
`docs/design.md` section 6 does not exist — issue #40.

## to-be

Holding a bound key records for as long as it is held. Releasing it ends the
take after the release gap, and the transcript is inserted into the pane the key
was held over, unsubmitted. A tap that was not a hold records nothing and says
so. No hold is ever ended by a signal the plugin could not read, and no take is
ever discarded because a duration came out impossible.

## requirements

1. **The release gap is one second.** It supersedes both the 250 milliseconds in
   `docs/design.md` section 5 and the five seconds in the ticket's comment. A gap
   in the repeat stream is not proof of release, and repeat delivery stutters
   under load — but one second is already about twelve times the 85 ms median
   repeat interval measured in `docs/evidence.md`, and about ten times the
   largest gap observed at the start of a hold, so it survives a stall an order
   of magnitude worse than anything measured. The cost of a longer gap is linear
   and paid on every take: a tail of recording, later text, more silence into
   recognition. The value is configurable, and the measurement that would settle
   it — the worst gap inside a hold on a loaded machine — is issue #56.
   `docs/design.md` is corrected by this task.
2. **A hold shorter than the minimum is a stray tap**, its recording discarded
   without transcription, and the person is told it was a tap rather than left
   with silence. The minimum is configurable; `docs/design.md` section 5 names
   300 milliseconds.
3. **Every file this mechanism introduces is written atomically** — written
   beside its destination and renamed — so that a reader sees the old content
   whole or the new content whole and never a truncated file. Measured in the
   prototype: writing by redirect produced 95 empty reads out of 755; writing
   and renaming produced 0 out of 1243.
4. **A signal that cannot be read or parsed is a fault, not a release.** The
   last good value is held, the fault is recorded, and a hold ends only on a
   value that is genuinely stale or on the signal disappearing — and the record
   says which of the two it was.
5. **An impossible duration never discards a take.** A negative or absurd hold
   length falls back to the clock, and the reason is recorded. In the prototype
   17 of 22 discarded takes had a negative duration, and the takes beside them
   lost 13, 34 and 41 seconds of speech.
6. **Cleanup knows its own process.** Nothing ends a recorder by pattern alone.
   A run that finds another recording in progress refuses to start rather than
   freeing the device by force.
7. **A lock is released only by its owner**, with one exception: a repair
   command that breaks it deliberately and records that it did.
8. **A keypress costs almost nothing.** The work done per repeat stays within
   the client's two-second reply bound with room to spare, and does no file
   system work that scales with the length of the hold.
9. **The tail is stated, not discovered.** Every hold carries up to a second of
   recording after the key is released, and the text arrives
   that much later. Where the plugin can say so — in its own output and in the
   configuration comment — it says so.

## What this stage hands to design, undecided

**Whether a file is involved at all.** The issue's rules are phrased in the
prototype's terms — a poke file, a watchdog polling it, a lock. That
architecture belongs to a shell script with no resident process. This plugin has
a daemon that is already resident and already receives every keypress as a
socket request. Design decides whether release is inferred from the arrival
times of those requests, held in memory, or from a file on disk. What does not
change with that choice is the invariant underneath rules 3 and 4: **a signal
the plugin could not read is never evidence that the key was released.** If the
mechanism introduces no file, rules 3 and 4 are satisfied by construction and
the artifact says so; if it introduces one, they apply literally.

**What ends a take that the daemon stopped hearing about.** The daemon holds the
device for its whole life. If pokes stop arriving because the client, herdr or
the machine wedged rather than because a key was released, something must still
end the take. Design names what.

**How `ptt` and the `dictate` toggle coexist.** The recorder answers
`AlreadyRunning` to a second start. A `ptt` hold beginning while a `dictate`
take is open, and a `dictate` arriving mid-hold, both need a defined answer.

**Where the hold is timed.** The recorder thread owns per-take state today and
the daemon owns none. Either can hold the stamps; the choice decides what a test
can reach.

**Whether `ptt` keeps the two-second client bound.** It is not `dictate`, so it
has the short bound today (`src/client.rs:32-36`). The keypress that ends a hold
is also the one whose reply arrives after recognition and delivery, unless
design separates them.

## acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Holding a bound key for longer than the minimum records for the
  duration of the hold, and the transcript is inserted into the pane the key was
  held over, unsubmitted.
- **AC-2** The take ends one second after the last keypress, not sooner. A gap
  shorter than a second inside a hold does not end it, and the take that
  results is one take, not two.
- **AC-3** The gap and the minimum hold are configuration keys with defaults, in
  a `[ptt]` section; an absent configuration file remains a valid state.
- **AC-4** A tap shorter than the minimum leaves no transcript, discards its
  recording, and produces a message saying it was too short to be a hold — not
  silence.
- **AC-5** A signal the plugin cannot read or parse does not end a hold. The
  hold continues on the last good value, and the journal records that the signal
  was unreadable.
- **AC-6** When a hold does end, the journal says whether it ended because the
  signal went stale or because it disappeared.
- **AC-7** A hold whose computed length is negative or impossible is recorded
  and delivered, never discarded. A test drives a clock backwards and shows the
  take surviving.
- **AC-8** Any file this mechanism writes is written beside its destination and
  renamed. A test reads the file concurrently with many writes and never
  observes a truncated or empty read. If the design introduces no such file, the
  artifact states that and this criterion is met by construction.
- **AC-9** A second run that finds a recording in progress refuses to start and
  says so, and ends nothing by pattern. No code path ends a process it did not
  start.
- **AC-10** A lock, if the design has one, is released only by the run that took
  it. The repair path that breaks it says in its output that it did.
- **AC-11** A `ptt` request is answered fast enough to be issued twelve times a
  second without queueing, within the client's reply bound, and does no work per
  repeat that grows with the length of the hold. Measured, not asserted.
- **AC-12** `ptt` and `dictate` have a defined interaction in both directions,
  covered by tests: a hold beginning while a toggle take is open, and a toggle
  arriving during a hold.
- **AC-13** Everything above is covered by tests that need no microphone, using
  the existing doubles — the scripted `Fake` source (`src/capture.rs:439`), the
  fake transcription engine, and the recording deliverer — plus whatever clock
  seam the design introduces, because a release gap cannot be waited out in a
  unit test — a second per case, across a dozen cases, on every run.
- **AC-14** `docs/design.md` section 5 states the release gap and the reason for
  it, and section 7's configuration sketch matches the keys that exist.
  `docs/decisions.md` gains a row for the gap.
- **AC-15** Verified by hand on macOS through a live herdr pane with a real key
  held: the hold records, the release ends it, and the text lands in the pane.
  Recorded in `docs/evidence.md` with the platform.

## Out of scope / noticed, not turned into a criterion

- **Trimming trailing silence before transcription.** Named in the issue's
  comment as worth considering here. Excluded by decision: it is audio
  processing, it interacts with issue #30, and it would hold this task open. Its
  own issue.
- **A key that ends a hold immediately.** Also named in the comment. Excluded by
  the same decision: it is a new user-visible name in the manifest and another
  key in the user's herdr configuration. Its own issue.
- **The indicator** (#40). This task must not depend on it and must not need it
  to be usable — but with a tail on every hold, a recording that is still running
  and says nothing is a worse experience than it was, and that is the argument
  for #40 rather than a criterion here.
- **`cancel` stopping anything** (#18). A hold cannot be cancelled today because
  `cancel` stops nothing; that stays true after this task.
- **`setup` printing the keybinding snippet** (#41). Without it the key has to
  be bound by hand, which is how AC-15 will be verified.
- **The rewrite defects** (#50, #51, #52) and the recognition ones. A take
  produced by a hold goes through the same pipeline as one produced by a toggle
  and inherits whatever that pipeline does.

## Risk

The measured facts are from a shell prototype polling files at 60 ms. This
plugin has a resident daemon and a socket. The prototype's numbers establish
that the defects are real and what they cost; they do not establish that the
same mechanism is the right one here. The risk is implementing the prototype's
architecture faithfully, files and all, when the daemon makes most of it
unnecessary — and inheriting a failure mode that would otherwise not exist.

The second risk is the release gap itself. It was chosen because losing a
take costs more than waiting, and that reasoning is settled. What it costs is a
tail on every hold, and with no indicator the person cannot tell a tail from a
hang.
