# AC_84

What recognition produced and what the rewrite made of it can both be read after
a take, and what a take leaves on disk when nobody asked for it is removed. Issue
#84 is the ticket, widened by its owner on 2026-09-18.

## as-is

**Which tree a line number is against.** Everything down to and including the
paragraph on the configuration is cited against `d83570c`, the commit this run was
cut from: S4 of this run moved several of those lines, and rewriting them to the
current tree would erase the problem the ticket is about rather than record it.
The material added when the owner widened the ticket — the measurement of what a
take costs, and the four endings that name a file — is cited against the working
tree, because it is what the implementer edits. Where one line differs between the
two, both are given.

**One line in the whole pipeline carries the take's words, and by the time it is
written the transcript is gone.** `src/daemon.rs:941` writes
`runtime.journal.write(&delivering_line(&text))`, and `delivering_line`
(`src/daemon.rs:1032-1034`) is `format!("delivering: {text}")`. The `text` it is
handed is the binding produced by the match at `src/daemon.rs:918-937`, which
shadows the transcript returned by recognition at `src/daemon.rs:892`. Nothing
between those two lines writes the earlier value anywhere. The transcript exists
in memory for the length of one function and is dropped when it returns.

**Four paths leave the transcript unrewritten, not three, and the line records
none of them.** At `src/daemon.rs:918-937`:

- `Resolution::Off` (line 919) — `[rewrite] engine = "off"`. The stage was
  switched off; nothing was attempted.
- `Resolution::Unavailable(why)` (lines 920-923) — a configured engine that did
  not resolve at start, or `"agent"`, which `src/rewrite.rs:80-85` folds into
  this variant. `tell_once` raises one notice per daemon, not per take.
- `skip::plain` (line 925) — the engine resolved and was deliberately not
  called, because the transcript is eight words or fewer with no run of two
  ASCII letters and no word shared with the bias (`src/rewrite/skip.rs:15-27`,
  `SKIP_WORD_LIMIT = 8`), and `skip_if_plain` defaults to true.
- A resolved engine whose `rewrite` call failed (lines 928-933) — the error is
  passed to `tell_once` and the transcript is delivered unchanged.

All four produce exactly the same `delivering:` line as a successful rewrite. The
ticket names two of them; the fourth is recorded here because a record that
cannot tell the four apart is the defect the ticket describes, and the fourth is
the one where a rewrite was attempted and did not work.

**The journal writes to standard error and nothing else.**
`src/daemon.rs:1019-1030`: the `Journal` trait has one production implementation,
`StderrJournal`, whose `write` is `eprintln!("{line}")`. The doc comment at
`src/daemon.rs:1005-1007` says herdr captures a plugin's standard error, so
`herdr plugin log list --plugin herdr-voice` shows it. That sentence is true of
an **action**, and the daemon is not an action.

**Measured, on macOS, 2026-09-18.** A throwaway plugin with its own id was linked,
its action invoked and the plugin unlinked again; the owner's installation was not
touched.

- An action's output is captured. The probe action's record came back with
  `"stdout": "PROBE84-ACTION-STDOUT\n"` and `"stderr": "PROBE84-ACTION-STDERR\n"`.
- **The plugin log is one ring of 200 records, shared by every installed plugin.**
  `herdr plugin log list --limit 10000` returned exactly 200 records with
  `log_id` running from `plugin-log-3365` to `plugin-log-3564` — 200 consecutive
  ids, no gap and no older entry — spread across three plugins.
- **Dictation alone fills that ring in under a minute.** Of those 200, 198 were
  this plugin's `ptt` action, and the 199 `ptt` records present a moment earlier
  spanned 43 seconds of wall clock. `ptt` fires on every key auto-repeat, about
  twelve times a second (`docs/design.md` section 2), and each repeat is one
  record.

So a record placed in the plugin log is overwritten by the next few seconds of
holding a key, whatever else is true of it. This is independent of where the
daemon's standard error goes, and it settles the plugin log as a destination.

**Where the daemon's standard error goes was not established for the herdr-started
case, and the reason is recorded rather than guessed.** The daemon running on this
machine has parent process 1 and its file descriptors 1 and 2 both point at a file
in the system temporary directory, which is a hand-made redirection and not
herdr's doing. Observing what herdr does with a `[[startup]]` process's standard
error requires restarting herdr, and the owner is dictating through it. Linking
the probe plugin did not start its `[[startup]]` entry — no process appeared and
no record was written — so a link alone cannot produce the observation either.

**A take already has a name, and nothing else identifies it.** `take_path`
(`src/capture.rs:405-411`) builds `<state>/takes/{unix-millis}-{pid}-{counter}.wav`,
and `Take` (`src/capture.rs:95-114`) carries `path`, `level_dbfs`, `target`,
`agent`, `cwd` and `tab` — no identifier of its own. The state directory is
`transport::state_directory` (`src/transport.rs:121-133`).

**The recording of a delivered take is never removed.** `std::fs::remove_file` on
a take's path appears twice outside tests: `src/capture.rs:401`, in `discard`, and
`src/daemon.rs:669` — 665 at `d83570c` — in `discard_take`, for a hold too short
to be one. Both are paths where the take
was refused, and neither names a path to anybody. A take that reached delivery
keeps its `.wav` for ever.

Measured on this machine on 2026-09-18: eight takes in the state directory,
3.4 MB in total, between 205 KB and 717 KB each. The format is 16 kHz mono
16-bit, which is 32 KB a second, so the largest of those is about 22 seconds of
speech — and a take of 21.5 seconds that afternoon was 700 KB, which agrees.

**Four endings already promise somebody the file by name**, and this is what the
deletion rule has to compose with:

- recognition unavailable — `"{why} — the take is kept at {path}"`,
  `src/daemon.rs:888-890`
- transcription failed — the same message, `src/daemon.rs:899-902`
- delivery refused — `"could not deliver to {target} ({why}) — the take is kept
  at {path}; text: ..."`, `src/daemon.rs:990`
- the daemon stopped with the key still down — `kept_on_shutdown_line`,
  `src/daemon.rs:1200-1206`. That take never enters the pipeline at all:
  `finish_on_shutdown` (`src/daemon.rs:551-565`) stops the recorder and journals
  the path, and `transcribe_take` is never called for it.

**The principle is already written down**, at `src/capture.rs:398`, above
`discard`: "A take nobody will read is a take nobody should find later." What is
missing is its other half — a take that *was* read, and is therefore finished
with.

**Adding a configuration key is a field with a default.** `Config`
(`src/config.rs:19-29`) is `#[serde(default)]` over one struct per section, each
itself `#[serde(default)]` with a `Default` impl; `load` (`src/config.rs:281`)
returns `Config::default()` for an absent or unreadable file. An absent
configuration file is already a valid state, and an unknown key in a file is
ignored rather than refused (`src/config.rs:1-8`).

## to-be

After a take has been dictated, a person can read, for that take and no other:
the transcript recognition returned, the text the rewrite returned, and whether
the rewrite ran — and when it did not, which of the ways it did not. The record
is somewhere a person can reach without a debugger and without redirecting a
process's standard error by hand, and it survives the next few seconds of
dictation.

Nothing of this happens unless a configuration key says so. The key is off when
no configuration file exists.

With that key off — the default — a take that was delivered leaves nothing
behind: its recording goes when the take is finished with, rather than sitting in
the state directory until somebody notices. With it on, the recording and the
record are both kept, and both are bounded.

## requirements

Asked for by the ticket:

- Both stages' output recorded per take, with the rewrite's status.
- Tied to the take, so two takes cannot be confused.
- Off by default, on by a configuration key.
- The key and what it writes documented in `docs/design.md` section 7.
- Tests for both stages appearing, and for the key being off writing neither.

Implied by the ticket, and treated as requirements here:

- Reachable. "A person who has just dictated can see" is not satisfied by a value
  that exists only in a stream nobody is reading. What the ticket leaves open is
  the destination, not whether it has to be findable.
- Local only. The ticket's "Out of bounds" says sending anything anywhere is not
  part of this.

Added by the owner on 2026-09-18, widening the ticket's "Out of bounds":

- A delivered take's recording is removed unless the key says to keep it. The
  ticket's text puts audio out of bounds; that text is superseded by this
  decision, and the criteria below are gated against the wider scope rather than
  the narrower one.

Bound by `CLAUDE.md` rather than by the ticket:

- No panic path. A record that cannot be written must not end the take.
- No silent failure. A record that cannot be written says so.

## acceptance criteria

**AC-1.** For a take whose rewrite ran, the record carries both the transcript
recognition returned and the text the rewrite returned, and the two are
distinguishable from each other in the record.

**AC-2.** The record says whether the rewrite ran. When it did not, the record
names which of the four ways: the stage is off, the engine is unavailable, the
skip heuristic decided the take needed no rewriting, or the engine was called and
failed.

**AC-3.** Every record names the take it belongs to, and two takes made in one
daemon's lifetime cannot be confused for one another.

**AC-4.** A person who has just dictated can reach the record by a path or a
command the plugin itself names, without redirecting the daemon's standard error
and without reading the shared plugin-log ring.

**AC-5.** Nothing of AC-1 to AC-3 is written when the key is off, and the key is
off when no configuration file exists.

**AC-6.** The key has a default like every other key, and an absent configuration
file remains a valid state: the daemon starts and dictation works unchanged.

**AC-7.** `docs/design.md` section 7 names the key beside the other configuration
and states what is written, where it is written, and what removes it.

**AC-8.** A test asserts that for a take that was rewritten, with the key on, both
the transcript and the rewritten text appear in the record.

**AC-9.** A test asserts that with the key off, neither appears.

**AC-10.** A test asserts that the record distinguishes a take that was rewritten
from one that was not, for each of the four ways it was not.

**AC-11.** Nothing is sent off the machine. The record is written locally and
read locally.

**AC-12.** A record that cannot be written does not end the take: the text is
still delivered, and the failure is reported rather than swallowed.

**AC-13.** With the key off, a take whose text was delivered leaves no recording
on disk.

**AC-14.** A take that ended any other way keeps its recording at the moment it
ends, whatever the key says. That is: recognition unavailable, transcription
failed, delivery refused, and the daemon stopping with the key still down — the
four endings whose message names the file by path. Keeping here is about the
take's own ending and about nothing else; it does not exempt the file from
AC-16.

**AC-15.** With the key on, a delivered take's recording is kept beside its
record.

**AC-16.** What accumulates in the takes directory is bounded, and the bound
applies whether the key is on or off. No more than a stated number of takes' files
are kept there, and the recording and the record of one take are kept or removed
together.

**AC-17.** Nothing is removed twice and nothing that was already removed is
removed again: the two existing refusal paths — `src/capture.rs:401` and
`src/daemon.rs:669` — keep sole responsibility for the takes they discard. Both
are against the working tree, which is what a criterion has to name.

## Ambiguity, and the reading taken

**"Whether the rewrite ran at all" — two ways or four.** The ticket names
`skip_if_plain` and an unavailable engine. The code has four paths that deliver an
unrewritten transcript, and the two the ticket does not name are the stage being
switched off and a resolved engine whose call failed. AC-2 and AC-10 take all
four. The reason is that the ticket's own purpose is telling apart which stage
made the text wrong, and "the engine was called and returned an error" is the one
case where a rewrite was attempted — grouping it with "the engine is off" would
put the most diagnostic of the four out of reach. This is the larger reading, and
it is written here rather than taken quietly.

**"Finished with" — the reading taken.** The owner's words are "delete the
recordings unless debug is on", which does not say when a take is finished with.
Delivery succeeding is not the only way a take ends. The reading taken is the
narrow one: a take is finished with when its text has been delivered, and on no
other path. Each of the other four endings already tells somebody where the file
is, by path, in the reply or in the journal — deleting there would turn a promise
into a pointer at nothing, which is worse than either behaviour on its own. A
delivery herdr refused therefore keeps its recording, although deleting it could
be argued for: that reply was built to carry the whole text precisely because it
reached neither the pane nor the screen (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-08-26, #22),
and the path it names is part of the same promise.

**The bound is a property of the directory, not of the key.** The gate asked
which files AC-16 covers, because two readings collided with AC-14. It covers
both kinds, keyed by the take they belong to, and it runs whether the key is on or
off. The reason is that with the key off the four endings of AC-14 still leave
recordings behind — a record is written before the delivery attempt, so a take
herdr refused has both files, while a take that failed recognition has only its
`.wav` — and a bound that lived inside the recording feature would leave exactly
that set unbounded, which is the accumulation the owner's widening is about.

This does not weaken AC-14. What that criterion promises is that a take's own
ending does not delete its recording: the message names a path, and the person who
reads that message goes and looks. It does not promise the file is there for ever,
and no message says it is.

**"Can see" — not settled here.** The ticket does not say where the record goes,
and the as-is above rules out one candidate by measurement rather than by
preference. AC-4 states the property the destination has to have; which
destination it is belongs to S2.

## What S2 must settle

- The destination, against AC-4.
- What removes the record, against AC-7. The plugin keeps every delivered take's
  audio with no retention rule today, so there is no existing answer to copy and
  one has to be chosen.
- The record's format, and how a take is named in it given that a take has a
  file path and no identifier of its own.
- Whether the bound of AC-16 counts takes or bytes, and why. The number that was
  chosen for records alone is now carrying audio that is three orders of
  magnitude larger: fifty records is tens of kilobytes, fifty recordings is tens
  of megabytes at the sizes measured above.
- Where the deletion sits, so that AC-17 holds by construction rather than by a
  check somebody has to remember to write.
- Where the bound of AC-16 lives, given that it can no longer be a method of the
  record writer: that type exists only when the key is on, and the bound has to
  apply when it is off.

## Out of scope / noticed

- **`tell_once` is per daemon, not per take.** `src/daemon.rs:1167-1185`: an
  unavailable engine or a failed call raises one notice for the life of the
  daemon. Somebody reading the record for a take made an hour after the first
  failure will find no notice anywhere. AC-2 makes the record carry the status
  itself, which removes the dependency; the notice's own frequency is not touched.
- ~~**The recording of a delivered take is never removed.**~~ Raised here on
  2026-09-18 as noticed and out of bounds; the owner widened the ticket the same
  day and it is now AC-13 to AC-17.
- **The `delivering:` line's own shape is unchanged.** Nothing here asks for it to
  move, be renamed or carry more.
